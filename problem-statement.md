# Problem Statement

## The Core Problem

Current blockchain systems are fundamentally vulnerable to quantum attacks due to their reliance on **classical cryptographic assumptions** that quantum computers are designed to break.

## Specific Challenges

### 1. Cryptographic Vulnerability

The vast majority of blockchain networks — including Bitcoin, Ethereum, and BNB Smart Chain — use elliptic curve digital signature algorithms (ECDSA) for transaction signing. A sufficiently powerful quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could:

- Derive private keys from exposed public keys
- Forge valid digital signatures
- Compromise wallet security across entire networks

### 2. Lack of Accessible Testing Tools

There is currently **no widely available, user-friendly platform** for blockchain developers to test the quantum resistance of their systems. Existing quantum simulation tools are:

- Highly specialized and academic in nature
- Expensive to access and operate
- Disconnected from blockchain-specific use cases

### 3. Industry Unpreparedness

Despite growing awareness of quantum risks, the blockchain industry remains largely unprepared:

- Most protocols have no post-quantum migration plan
- Smart contracts are not audited for quantum vulnerability
- Developers lack the knowledge and tools to assess quantum risk

### 4. Centralized Quantum Access

Current quantum computing resources are controlled by a small number of corporations and research institutions. This creates:

- **Barriers to entry** for smaller projects and independent researchers
- **Single points of failure** in quantum-dependent security testing
- **Geographic and economic inequality** in access to quantum capabilities

## The Gap

There is a critical need for a **decentralized, blockchain-native platform** that makes quantum security testing accessible, affordable, and verifiable. VeriQ fills this gap.
